# NILA VARNAM — Reference Moodboard Interpretation

This is the intended design interpretation of the user-supplied stationery and material moodboards.

## Brand feeling

The brand should feel tactile, considered and quietly confident. Think of an independent art house whose identity has been shaped by the materials and tools of making art: paper, canvas, pigment, brush, natural fibre, wood, ink and hand-finished detail.

It must look contemporary enough for a boutique hotel buyer or corporate gifting manager, but it should retain a trace of Indian and Kerala cultural warmth.

## Colour hierarchy

### Deep teal — brand anchor
Use for strong brand moments, navigation/wordmark contexts, selected section backgrounds, small labels and visual contrast. It should feel rich and mature rather than bright or digital.

### Warm ivory / parchment — main surface
This should occupy most of the website. Avoid pure white as the default background. Use subtle paper-like warmth while maintaining readable contrast.

### Kraft / ochre / natural brown — material accent
Use for supporting surfaces, image frames, fine details and editorial blocks. It should suggest natural paper, wood, packaging and handwork.

### Stone / taupe / muted neutral
Use for secondary surfaces, dividers, quiet captions and supporting UI.

### Restrained red — attention accent
Red is an intentional attention cue requested by the owner. It is not the brand anchor. Use it sparingly:
- One primary conversion button in a key viewport, if appropriate.
- A tiny active marker or underline.
- A small callout for a deadline or enquiry action.
- A selected state with sufficient contrast.

Do not use red for every CTA, all links, repeated icons or large backgrounds.

## Texture

The reference contains tactile paper, kraft, fibres and fabric-like material. Translate these into the web through:
- Real photography of paper, artwork and studio materials.
- Very subtle background grain only if it does not harm performance or text clarity.
- Fine borders and material-toned panels.
- Carefully composed close-up photography.

Do not overlay heavy textures on top of artwork. Do not simulate texture so aggressively that the site feels vintage or crafty.

## Layout character

The flatlay reference is orderly, composed and quiet. The expanded palette reference uses a sequence of swatches and organic shapes. Translate that into:
- Well-aligned editorial grids.
- One or two asymmetrical compositions per page.
- Large uninterrupted artwork images.
- Strong, deliberate whitespace.
- Occasional organic shape or crop, not an entire interface full of decorative blobs.

## Photography

Actual paintings should be presented as art objects. Use accurate colours, neutral backgrounds and high-resolution photography. Avoid placing every painting inside the same mock frame.

## Motion

The mood suggests calm, tactile transitions:
- Slow but not sluggish image reveals.
- Small opacity/position transitions.
- A soft gallery lightbox.
- Restrained hover feedback.
- No exaggerated scroll theatre.

## What this is not

- Not a red-and-white brand.
- Not a generic teal SaaS template.
- Not a Kerala tourism website.
- Not a handicrafts ecommerce site.
- Not a faux-luxury black-and-gold brand.
- Not a cluttered portfolio template.
